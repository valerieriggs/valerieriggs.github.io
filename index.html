About

At Morgan State University, I teach undergraduate and graduate courses in teacher education, including Artificial Intelligence for Educators, a course I developed. I design and lead AI training for university faculty and Maryland teachers, and I build AI capacity at universities abroad, most recently as a Fulbright Specialist in Equatorial Guinea. As affiliated faculty with the NSF Institute for Trustworthy AI in Law & Society (TRAILS), I lead funded research on AI tutoring and bias in AI. I also advise doctoral students and mentor undergraduate and graduate researchers.

Research and projects

PI · NSF TRAILS

GUARD

Ground, Unbundle, Anchor, Resolve, Distill · 2026
NSF Award No. 2229885

Large language models often describe HBCUs by their category instead of their actual record. GUARD is a prompt-level method that corrects this. In testing, it eliminated the failure pattern entirely, cutting it from 51% of responses to 0%. It is freely available as a skill for Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini, and was built with a team of Morgan State doctoral and undergraduate researchers.

PI · NSF TRAILS

Human-Enabled AI Tutoring

2026 · New Orleans · $25,000

Roughly two-thirds of U.S. fourth graders do not read at grade level, and many teachers are wary of AI tools in their classrooms. This project tackles both problems. At Dorothy Height Charter School (Crescent City Schools), an adaptive AI reading tutor grounded in the Science of Reading was paired with trained mentor-tutors at a 1:4 ratio, giving K–5 students high-dosage, small-group support during the school day.

Working with the National Center for the Elimination of Educational Disparities, Meta Connected Tutoring and Amira Learning, I studied how educators came to trust the model. A survey of 71 teachers and staff, together with interviews with school leaders before and after implementation, showed that trust grew mostly from watching the human-AI pairing work, not from system data.

Co-PI · NSF TRAILS

Better Together

2025–2026 · Baltimore · Two awards

Better Together builds AI capacity by pairing mentor teachers with the teacher candidates they supervise, so the two learn AI side by side instead of alone. Over a series of workshops, pairs explored what AI can and cannot do, practiced verifying AI-generated content, and designed interdisciplinary lessons together. They presented their final projects in September 2026.

Drawing on hope theory and fuzzy-set analysis, the research tracks how partnership shapes teacher confidence, agency, trust in AI, adoption and implementation barriers. The project has been presented at AERA and the TRAILS conference.

Co-Investigator · NSF TRAILS

AI Literacy for K–12 Educators: Ethical Use of AI in Teaching

June 2025 · Morgan State University

A three-day professional development workshop for PK–12 teachers that put trustworthiness at the center of AI use. Rather than telling teachers which tools to trust, the workshop helped them set their own criteria for deciding when, why and whether to use generative AI in planning, instruction, assessment and communication with families.

Teachers explored tools such as ChatGPT and NotebookLM, examined how AI interprets data and where bias enters, wrote classroom AI guidelines, and built a final AI-integrated project scored against a rubric based on the MAILS AI literacy scale. The research team studied changes in teachers' AI literacy and self-efficacy, and the design was accepted as a paper at the 2026 AERA Annual Meeting.

PI (Morgan State) · Sloan Foundation

Morgan State–University at Buffalo AI Pathways

2025–present · With the University at Buffalo (SUNY)

A cross-institutional collaboration to build a pathway from undergraduate study into graduate education and research in computer science, data science and engineering. Following a 2025 Sloan Foundation planning grant and reciprocal campus visits, the partners are piloting a cross-institutional research exchange, an AI Design Studio and cohort-based mentoring, and designing a shared 4+1 (BS+MS) program.

I lead Morgan's student mentoring and graduate pathway work. The collaboration also studies participatory AI curriculum design, in which students and faculty co-design modules that pair technical AI instruction with ethics and social context. This work has been presented internationally and is under review at IEEE Transactions on Education.

Summer Program Coordinator, High School Teachers · CEAMLS

CEAMLS SARI Summer Institute

2024–2025 · NSF-funded · Maryland high school teachers

For two summers I designed and led the AI literacy strand of Morgan's CEAMLS Summer AI Research Institute for public school teachers from under-resourced Maryland schools. Teachers joined university AI research teams for the summer while attending a workshop series I built on the Digital Promise AI literacy framework and AI4K12's Five Big Ideas in AI. The workshops covered AI foundations, data privacy and trustworthiness, and integrating AI into the high school curriculum. Each teacher wrote classroom AI-use guidelines and presented a final project showing how they would bring AI into their own teaching.

The institute produced three peer-reviewed articles, listed under Selected publications.

Global education

I lead study abroad programs that examine education systems in other countries, connecting undergraduates with schools and universities abroad.

Ghana

2021 · 2024 · 2025 · 2026

Undergraduates studied Ghana's education system from primary school through university. They attended lectures at the University of Ghana on colonialism and its impact on Ghanaian education, and at the University of Cape Coast comparing basic education in the U.S. and Ghana. They worked with students at Franphil International School, joined after-school projects with the Fafali Organization, and reflected daily through guided WhatsApp prompts. The program also included collaboration with All Nations University.

Costa Rica

2022 · 2024

Undergraduates observed and assisted classroom teachers in urban, rural and remote communities, including San José, Limón and Guanacaste. They learned from educators teaching multicultural and Latino K–12 students, with the goal of strengthening teaching for Black K–12 students in the United States. Earlier trips placed students with host families and in dual-language elementary classrooms.

Japan

2025 · Tokyo, Kyoto and Kamakura

Undergraduates explored teaching and learning in Japan, especially for marginalized communities. They visited schools, attended a lecture on Japan's education system, connected with the Black community in Japan, and reflected daily on identity and teaching through guided WhatsApp prompts.

Equatorial Guinea

Fulbright Specialist · 2026

As a Fulbright Specialist, I spent three weeks building institutional capacity at the Afro-American University of Central Africa (AAUCA) in Djibloho, a new Pan-African university that graduated its first class in 2025. I worked across the university with students, faculty, administrative staff and senior leadership, leading a six-day AI program on trust, ethics and discipline-specific use, with Morgan State faculty joining through live COIL exchanges. The work produced an executive summary for university leadership and new research connections on building AI datasets in Fang and Bubi.

The study abroad program received the 2025 Department of the Year Award for Study Abroad Program Impact.
